# MediaPipe shoe evidence experiment

This development experiment uses the official MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Heavy model and its 33 image landmarks, including ankle, heel and foot index. Model world-coordinate outputs are not used for physical measurements. The primary references are the [official Python guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/python) and [model/landmark overview](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/index).

Kane was inspected as a pilot before the 39-event cohort. IMAGE mode produced anatomical-channel jumps; an exploratory VIDEO pilot suggested better temporal continuity, but used a moving ROI. Those exploratory outputs were superseded before the full cohort. Fixed-union-ROI pilots retain unknown on the course clip, and a right shoe-swing candidate with unknown contact region on the additional FIFA clip. This motivated evaluating the fixed method across the cohort and makes the experiment development work, not a held-out test. No reviewer labels, known player footedness or outcome labels enter the rule.

Each normal and horizontally reflected ROI has its own independent MediaPipe VIDEO instance. Both IMAGE and VIDEO use the same fixed union of the event's planned source ROIs, without moving or scaling the crop between frames. Crop coordinates are restored to source pixels; the reflected anatomical channels are swapped using the official 33-point topology. Tracking does not cross an event or shot boundary. ROIs come from the original qualified source-only person inference. A predicted source body box must match that ROI through the existing anchor-overlap/centre/area gates. Low-confidence or rejected identity frames cannot enter the rule. Source decoder FPS, dimensions and frame count must match provenance; decoded frame IDs/timestamps and per-view monotonically increasing VIDEO timestamps are checked.

Original same-frame rival actors are supplied to the ROI identity gate. Predicted heel/toe points outside the crop are marked missing, with their coordinates retained for audit rather than clamped to an image border.

The inherited paired leg-motion rule uses a 0.4 confidence threshold. Torso identity and heel/toe support for the shoe-region frame use 0.5. The protocol field `landmark_visibility_presence_min=0.5` describes the latter checks; it is not a global minimum for every landmark entering the motion rule.

The fixed strike-window rule runs twice: using ankles, and using heel/foot-index centroids instead of ankles. The latter must pass unchanged paired confidence, mirror, anatomical continuity, motion ratio and stationary-region approach gates. A contact-region candidate additionally needs at least one same source frame in the contact bracket where both views have confident heel-to-foot-index segments within 0.08 preparation body heights of the last actual stationary ball point. That frame must also pass the paired pose gate. The supporting foot can be nearer the ball, so distance alone never selects the anatomical side. Any local ball identity ambiguity keeps both outputs unknown.

The 39-event cohort uses existing source-bound contact candidates and source-qualified person ROIs. Missing independent contact stays unknown. Its parameters are frozen before the cohort run, with source/video/contact/ROI/model/code hashes recorded. The additional FIFA edit is a same-event corroboration pilot, not an independent match or accuracy denominator.

Outputs are image-coordinate shoe-swing and shoe-region candidates. They are not directly observed contact labels. MediaPipe is a general human pose model; predicted heel and foot-index landmarks are not verified shoe outlines. Whole-person detection availability must be reported separately from accepted ROIs and reliable foot evidence.
